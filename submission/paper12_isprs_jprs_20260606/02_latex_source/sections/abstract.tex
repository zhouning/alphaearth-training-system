\begin{abstract}
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) is widely used to adapt large pre-trained models, but its behavior on geospatial foundation models remains insufficiently characterized when sensor inputs, spectral channels, and deployment domains differ from pre-training. This paper studies that question for one widely used backbone, Prithvi-100M. We evaluate seven adaptation strategies across five heterogeneous input configurations on EuroSAT, validate the main ranking on BigEarthNet-S2, extend the protocol to LandCover.ai semantic segmentation, and test whether the ranking survives a production-style Linhe County land-use/land-cover workflow and LoveDA urban/rural cross-domain transfer. The study covers 109 public-benchmark runs, a 35{,}920-patch Linhe corpus supervised by Esri-derived land-cover labels, and a 30-run LoveDA cross-domain sweep.

The results support three bounded conclusions for Prithvi-100M. First, standard module-wise LoRA is vulnerable to a fused-QKV implementation trap in PyTorch-style attention: it misses the packed query, key, and value projections and behaves like a linear-probe baseline. A diagnostic split-QKV variant improves EuroSAT s2\_full accuracy from 0.658 to 0.707, but remains below Houlsby adapters and does not close the gap under harder RGB and cross-domain settings. Second, Houlsby adapters provide the most reliable adaptation among the evaluated PEFT methods, reaching 94.5\% of the full fine-tuning score on EuroSAT s2\_full while training 1.4\% of the parameters, and widening the advantage on LandCover.ai dense prediction. Third, input modality and domain shift strongly condition the usefulness of small adapters: spectral richness affects accuracy more than PEFT choice on the public classification benchmarks, whereas the Linhe and LoveDA experiments indicate that label quality, geographic split, and adapter capacity govern performance under deployment-like conditions.

These findings do not establish a universal PEFT ranking for all geospatial foundation models. They show that architecture-aware inspection is necessary before transferring PEFT intuitions from mainstream vision to Prithvi-like remote sensing backbones, and they identify the additional evidence needed to generalize the ranking beyond this backbone.
\end{abstract}
