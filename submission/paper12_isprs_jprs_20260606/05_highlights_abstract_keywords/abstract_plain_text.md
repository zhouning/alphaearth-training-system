# Plain-Text Abstract

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) is widely used to adapt large pre-trained models, but its behavior on geospatial foundation models remains insufficiently characterized when sensor inputs, spectral channels, and deployment domains differ from pre-training. This paper studies that question for Prithvi-100M. We evaluate seven adaptation strategies across five heterogeneous input configurations on EuroSAT, validate the main ranking on BigEarthNet-S2, extend the protocol to LandCover.ai semantic segmentation, and test whether the ranking survives a production-style Linhe County land-use/land-cover workflow and LoveDA urban/rural cross-domain transfer. The study covers 109 public-benchmark runs, a 35,920-patch Linhe corpus supervised by Esri-derived land-cover labels, and a 30-run LoveDA cross-domain sweep.

The results support three bounded conclusions for Prithvi-100M. Standard module-wise LoRA is vulnerable to a fused-QKV implementation trap in PyTorch-style attention, while corrected split-QKV LoRA improves but remains below Houlsby adapters in the tested settings. Houlsby adapters provide the most reliable adaptation among the evaluated PEFT methods, reaching 94.5% of the full fine-tuning score on EuroSAT s2_full while training 1.4% of the parameters. Input modality and domain shift strongly condition the usefulness of small adapters: spectral richness affects accuracy more than PEFT choice on public classification benchmarks, whereas Linhe and LoveDA indicate that label quality, geographic split, and adapter capacity govern performance under deployment-like conditions. These findings do not establish a universal PEFT ranking for all geospatial foundation models, but they show that architecture-aware inspection is necessary before transferring PEFT intuitions from mainstream vision to Prithvi-like remote sensing backbones.
